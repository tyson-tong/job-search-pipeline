# How jobflow's rules work

The rules behind merging duplicates, flagging similar jobs, scoring, checking tailored CVs and cover letters, and ranking skill gaps. For a diagram of every component and table, open [structure.html](structure.html) in a browser.

## Duplicates

Each scraper's output is first converted to the same fields (`src/jobflow/ingest/sources.py`). A job then counts as already seen if any of these match, in order:
1. Same site and job ID (or, for a posting without an ID, the same URL without tracking parameters), checked against every site's copy seen so far, not just the first.
2. Identical company, title, location, and description (its first 1,500 characters, ignoring case and punctuation), in the same country (an unknown country doesn't block a match).
3. Same employer and title, ignoring suffixes like "Pty Ltd" or "Pte Ltd", in the same city and country, first seen in the last 90 days, where one ad is a cut-down or padded copy of the other: at least 95% of the shorter ad's words are in the longer one (and, for an ad under 30 distinct words, the two share at least 80% of their words). This catches the same job on LinkedIn, Indeed, and Seek, where a site may cut an ad short or add a line, and reposts of the same ad with a new ID. Sharing words isn't enough: an employer's standard text (about us, benefits, diversity) can be most of two different roles' ads, while each role's own duties keep it under 95%. "Same city" only rules out a match when both postings name different places, such as Sydney and Melbourne; a missing or country-only location doesn't. Suburbs count as different places, so a Parramatta and a Sydney listing of one job stay separate (the tracker lists them as similar jobs).
4. The same title (ignoring case and punctuation), in the same city and country, first seen in the last 90 days, with an ad at least 95% the same words, whatever the employer's spelling. This catches one ad listed under slightly different employer names. "Same city" works as in rule 3: a missing or country-only location doesn't rule out a match, but two listings naming different places do, so one employer's identical ad for its Sydney and Melbourne offices stays two jobs. Short ads (under 30 distinct words) never match this way, a different title never does (employers reuse one ad for different roles), and neither does one ad for offices in two countries. A job's country is the one its search run covered, or else the country named in its location; an unknown country doesn't block a match.

The job keeps the first site's details (and the longest description). Each site's copy, with its own ID and link, is kept in the `job_sources` table, so later sightings match by ID and you can open the job on any site it was listed on. Every sighting is also recorded with the scraper run it came from; its full scraped record is kept for 30 days, then dropped to save space (the job keeps its first copy's record). An archived job's ad and scraped record are cleared 90 days after it was archived, by when the merge no longer compares its ad (rules 3 and 4 look back 90 days from first seen); its sites' IDs and fingerprint still recognise a repost. A similar-job note, which looks back 180 days, stops mentioning it then.

## Similar jobs

Merging is kept strict so two different jobs aren't combined, which means some copies stay separate: a repost with an edited ad, a recruiter's re-post, a reworded title, or a suburb instead of the city. As a safety net, the tracker compares each job with every job first seen in the last 180 days in the same country, ignoring employer and city: titles sharing at least 60% of their words and ads sharing at least 40% are listed as similar jobs, and as highly similar when the ads share at least 80%. They are never merged automatically. The same ad in another country (one role advertised for offices in Sydney and Hong Kong, say: titles sharing at least 60% of their words and ads at least 80%) is not listed as similar: it is placed next to the job in the list with a blue bar (similar jobs get an orange one), tagged "Same ad in" that country, and listed separately in the job panel.

## Archiving

Jobs leave the tracker's tabs, to status `archived`, when you're plainly done with them: a Filtered job 2 weeks past its posted date (or the date first seen, if later), a job 2 weeks after you marked it Not interested, and an Inbox job you haven't triaged once it was posted 30 days ago and has been in the Inbox that long (a job moved back to the Inbox starts again). The daily import and `jobflow web` do this. An archived job stays in the database and still matches as a duplicate, so a repost stays out of the Inbox; its ad is cleared 90 days later (see above).

## Match scores

The LLM lists the job's requirements, marks each required or preferred, and labels it matched, partial, or not evidenced in each CV, with CV quotes. Every quote is checked against the CV file. A "matched" item whose quote isn't found becomes "partial", and a "partial" one becomes "not evidenced". "Not evidenced" means the CV doesn't show it, not that you lack it. The score is calculated, not asked of the LLM: matched counts 1, partial 0.5, not evidenced 0, averaged within required and within preferred, then weighted by `required_weight` and `preferred_weight` in `config/profile.toml`. Changing the weights re-ranks every stored score with no LLM call, in the tracker and in `tailor-cv` alike. Use scores for ranking, not as a prediction.

Which jobs are scored: every Interested job (**Score all jobs**), the Inbox jobs you pick with **Score selected**, to help you triage, and an unscored job you're pursuing (interested, applied, interviewing, or offered) that `tailor-cv` or `cover-letter` works on. Not interested, filtered, archived, and closed jobs never are. A job is scored once against each CV, by its short name (the file name without its extension and a leading `CV_`), and keeps its scores when its status changes. A CV added to `cvs` or renamed counts as a new CV: each Interested job is scored against it the next time you score or tailor it, and keeps its other scores. Scores against a CV no longer named in `cvs` are kept in the database but neither shown nor used. A CV over 28,000 characters (as text) isn't scored at all, rather than scored on its start.

Editing a CV doesn't rescore anything by itself: the tracker marks each score made against an earlier version of the CV, and **Rescore edited CVs** scores the jobs you're pursuing (interested, applied, interviewing, or offered) again against only the CVs you've edited; closed jobs keep their scores. For a YAML CV only its `cv` section is scored, so only a change there counts as an edit, not a RenderCV design, locale, or settings change; for a `.pdf`, `.docx`, or `.tex`, only a change to its text (in a `.tex`, outside comments and commands).

## Tailored CVs

Only the CV's `cv` section goes to the LLM; design, locale, and settings are copied from your file. The draft is then compared with your original CV. These count as errors, and the CV is not rendered as a PDF or written as `--format`:
- a changed, added, or removed header field (name, contact details, and so on; the headline may be reworded, but is checked for numbers and terms like the rest)
- new or altered employers, positions, dates, or education
- an entry whose employer, position, dates, and education details don't match one entry of the original together, such as an end date taken from a later job or one employer's title given to another
- a removed employer, role (each position and start date at an employer), or education entry
- an entry's location changed (it may be left out)
- numbers that aren't in the original, and, in an entry, numbers from another entry of the original (a "30%" from one job given to another); a number in the headline or a section without employers or dates, such as skills, only has to be somewhere in the original
- markup that isn't in the original and that RenderCV would pass to Typst rather than print as text: a command such as `#text(fill: white)[...]` (text hidden in the PDF), `$$ math $$`, or a Markdown link, whose address the PDF doesn't show. A job ad could steer the LLM into adding one.

New capitalised or technical terms, such as a tool name, are warnings. The first word of a sentence or bullet is capitalised anyway, so it counts only when it starts a list ("Kubernetes, Python, SQL"), looks technical (AWS, PyTorch, Node.js), or is a term from the job ad, the likeliest to be claimed without support. The check catches common problems; it does not replace reading the draft.

With `--format docx` or `latex`, the CV file is laid out in code from the checked YAML (`candidate/documents.py`), never by the LLM, so it says exactly what passed the check. Like RenderCV, these layouts never split an entry across pages. A draft with errors gets no PDF and no `--format` file: fix the YAML and render it yourself with `uv run rendercv render CV_<variant>_draft.yaml` in its folder (a `.tex` compiles with lualatex). For a job tailored before, `--format docx` or `latex` just adds that file, laid out from its YAML draft after checking it again (in case you edited it), with no LLM call; if you've edited the draft since that file was written, it's written again.

The length is always measured on RenderCV's PDF, which is written beside the YAML draft, so a `.docx` or `.tex` is fitted to RenderCV's layout and its own page count may differ. If that PDF runs past 1 page, or the `--max-pages N` given to `tailor-cv`, the LLM ranks the bullets from least to most relevant to the job, and the fewest of the least relevant that makes it fit are cut, found by re-rendering with different numbers cut, in a temporary folder (a binary search, so about 9 renders even for 50 bullets). If it can't fit even with every entry down to one bullet, all those cuts are kept and you get a warning. The LLM only ranks; the cutting is done in code, so nothing is rewritten and every entry keeps at least one bullet. `CV_REVIEW.md` lists what was cut.

Since an entry is never split, one too long for the space left moves whole to the next page. A page other than the last that is at least 30% empty at the bottom because of this gets a warning (for RenderCV's layout); cutting a few of that entry's bullets pulls it up.

## Cover letters and the review

The cover letter is written from your best-matching CV, in any format (`--cv` picks another), and may draw only on that CV, the job ad, and the company research. A number found in none of them is an error; a new capitalised or technical term is a warning, as is a `[placeholder]` left in. A letter with errors is not written as `cover-letter.docx`; its text is in `COVER_LETTER_REVIEW.md` to fix. Cover letters are optional (`jobflow cover-letter`). For both CVs and letters, a reviewer prompted as the job's hiring manager critiques the first draft and revises it. The revision is checked the same way and kept only if it has no more errors than the first draft; otherwise the first draft is kept and the review file (`CV_REVIEW.md` or `COVER_LETTER_REVIEW.md`) says so. A review that fails (a network error, say) keeps the first draft, and the review file says so; but if the reviewer's key or model is wrong, or its credit has run out, the command stops, rather than write an unreviewed draft for every job.

## Skill gaps

Each scored job you're pursuing now (interested, applied, interviewing, or offered) contributes the requirements its best-matching CV didn't fully show; jobs still in the Inbox, and ones rejected, withdrawn, declined, or accepted, don't count. The LLM only groups them into skills (the same skill worded differently) and suggests how to close each gap; it can't change the counts. A skill's rank is the sum over its requirements of 1 for required or 0.5 for preferred, halved when the CV partly shows it. Ids the LLM makes up or repeats are ignored.

## Failures

If one Apify run fails, the error is recorded in the `scraper_runs` table, with the search name and the exact input, and the other runs still go ahead, unless the error is your Apify account's (a token Apify rejects, or the account's monthly usage limit reached): every run would fail the same way, so the runs not yet started are skipped. A run that succeeded on Apify but whose results couldn't be saved is recorded as `IMPORT_FAILED`, with its dataset ID: the results are still on Apify. A single posting that can't be read or that the database rejects is skipped (and named in the log) rather than failing the rest of its run; if every posting in a run fails, the run is recorded as `IMPORT_FAILED` instead. A run still going after `APIFY_MAX_WAIT_MINUTES` is stopped, so it can't keep charging for results that won't be imported; Apify stops it by itself too, so that holds even if the import is cut off (GitHub stops the daily job after 180 minutes). So that never happens, no run is started that could still be going 150 minutes after the import began (the 30 minutes left are for saving the last results and the upkeep): such a run isn't started, and is recorded as `NOT_STARTED` (each site's runs go one after another, so a site with many terms and places is the likeliest to reach it). The GitHub workflow then shows as failed, so you get GitHub's failure email.
