# How jobflow's rules work

Every rule jobflow follows, in one place: merging duplicates, flagging similar jobs, archiving, scoring, checking tailored CVs and cover letters, ranking skill gaps, which jobs each command does, what a change to your searches does to your jobs, and what the tracker flags. The README and [structure.html](structure.html) link here rather than repeat these; the settings themselves are in [searches.md](searches.md).

A job you're **pursuing** is one at Interested, Applied, Interviewing, or Offered.

## Duplicates

Each scraper's output is first converted to the same fields (`src/jobflow/ingest/sources.py`). A job then counts as already seen if any of these match, in order:
1. Same site and job ID (or, for a posting without an ID, the same URL without tracking parameters), checked against every site's copy seen so far, not just the first.
2. Identical company, title, location, and description (its first 1,500 characters, ignoring case and punctuation), in the same country (an unknown country doesn't block a match).
3. Same employer and title, ignoring suffixes like "Pty Ltd" or "Pte Ltd", in the same city and country, first seen in the last 90 days (the 20 most recent such jobs), where one ad is a cut-down or padded copy of the other: at least 95% of the shorter ad's words are in the longer one (and, for an ad under 30 distinct words, the two share at least 80% of their words; here and below, ads are compared by their words of 3 or more letters). This catches the same job on LinkedIn, Indeed, and Seek, where a site may cut an ad short or add a line, and reposts of the same ad with a new ID. Sharing words isn't enough: an employer's standard text (about us, benefits, diversity) can be most of two different roles' ads, while each role's own duties keep it under 95%. "Same city" only rules out a match when both postings name different places, such as Sydney and Melbourne; a missing or country-only location doesn't. Suburbs count as different places, so a Parramatta and a Sydney listing of one job stay separate (the tracker lists them as similar jobs).
4. The same title (ignoring case and punctuation other than `+` and `#`, so "C++ Developer" and "C# Developer" are two titles, in this rule and rule 3), in the same city and country, first seen in the last 90 days (the 50 most recent such jobs), with an ad at least 95% the same words, whatever the employer's spelling. This catches one ad listed under slightly different employer names. "Same city" works as in rule 3: a missing or country-only location doesn't rule out a match, but two listings naming different places do, so one employer's identical ad for its Sydney and Melbourne offices stays two jobs. Short ads (under 30 distinct words of 3 or more letters) never match this way, a different title never does (employers reuse one ad for different roles), and neither does one ad for offices in two countries. A job's country is the one its search run covered, or else the country named in its location; an unknown country doesn't block a match.

The job keeps the first site's details (and the longest description). Each site's copy, with its own ID and link, is kept in the `job_sources` table, so later sightings match by ID and you can open the job on any site it was listed on. Every sighting is also recorded with the scraper run it came from; its full scraped record is kept for 30 days, then dropped to save space (the job keeps its first copy's record). An archived job's ad and scraped record are cleared 90 days after it was archived, by when the merge no longer compares its ad (rules 3 and 4 look back 90 days from first seen); its sites' IDs and fingerprint still recognise a repost. A similar-job note, which looks back 180 days, stops mentioning it then.

## Similar jobs

Merging is kept strict so two different jobs aren't combined, which means some copies stay separate: a repost with an edited ad, a recruiter's re-post, a reworded title, or a suburb instead of the city. As a safety net, the tracker compares each job with every job first seen in the last 180 days in the same country, ignoring employer and city: titles sharing at least 60% of their words and ads sharing at least 40% are listed as similar jobs, and as highly similar when the ads share at least 80%. They are never merged automatically. The same ad in another country (one role advertised for offices in Sydney and Hong Kong, say: titles sharing at least 60% of their words and ads at least 80%) is not listed as similar: it is placed next to the job in the list with a blue bar (similar jobs get an orange one), tagged "Same ad in" that country, and listed separately in the job panel.

## Changing your searches

`jobflow update-searches` first checks `config/searches.toml`: an invalid file stops it before anything else, so a typo never reaches the daily import or your jobs. Then it does two separate things, each going ahead even if the other fails (no GitHub CLI, say), after which the command ends with an error naming what to fix before running it again:

- **Your jobs.** Every filter in [searches.md](searches.md#which-site-applies-which-filter) is applied to the jobs already in your Inbox, judging each by the search that found it first, as on the day it was imported (so a posted-date window never filters a job just for having aged since). If that search has since been renamed or removed, the job is judged by the exclusions under `[defaults]`. A job you moved out of the Filtered or Archived tab yourself is never filtered again, even if it later returns to the Inbox. The other way round, a Filtered job not yet archived comes back to the Inbox when the search that found it would no longer filter it, judged again by every filter, from the job as first scraped; if that search has been renamed or removed, only a job filtered by an excluded title term or employer can come back, judged by `[defaults]`. A job filtered for its posted date never comes back: the window it was judged by isn't stored, and may have been a one-off `jobflow ingest --posted-within-days`.
- **The daily import.** It publishes the file, and the timezone in `config/profile.toml`, as the `JOBFLOW_SEARCHES` and `JOBFLOW_TIMEZONE` GitHub variables the daily import reads.

Imports apply the filters only to the jobs they bring in; a job found by several searches is judged by the one that found it first, and later sightings aren't filtered again. `--dry-run` lists the jobs it would filter or bring back and changes nothing, on GitHub or in your database; if the database tables are out of date (after you update jobflow), it says so and stops, as only a real run brings them up to date.

## Archiving

Jobs move to the tracker's Archived tab, status `archived`, when you're plainly done with them: a Filtered job 2 weeks past its posted date (or the date first seen, if later), a job 2 weeks after you marked it Not interested, and an Inbox job you haven't triaged once it was posted 30 days ago and has been in the Inbox that long (a job moved back to the Inbox starts again). The daily import and `jobflow web` do this. An archived job still matches as a duplicate, so a repost stays out of the Inbox; its ad is cleared 90 days later (see above), after which it can't be scored or worked on: if you bring it back, scoring and the commands skip it. From the Archived tab you can mark a job Interested or move it back to the Inbox, where it starts its 30 days again; `update-searches` then never filters it (see above).

## Match scores

The LLM lists the job's requirements, marks each required or preferred, and labels it matched, partial, or not evidenced in each CV, with CV quotes. Every quote is checked against the CV file. A "matched" item whose quote isn't found becomes "partial", and a "partial" one becomes "not evidenced". "Not evidenced" means the CV doesn't show it, not that you lack it. The score is calculated, not asked of the LLM: matched counts 1, partial 0.5, not evidenced 0, averaged within required and within preferred, then weighted by `required_weight` and `preferred_weight` in `config/profile.toml`. Changing the weights re-ranks every stored score with no LLM call, in the tracker and in `tailor-cv` alike. Use scores for ranking, not as a prediction.

Which jobs are scored: every job you're pursuing (**Score all jobs**, two jobs at a time, or `tailor-cv` and `cover-letter` for a job they work on), and the Inbox jobs you pick with **Score selected**, to help you triage; a job keeps its score when you mark it Interested. Not interested, filtered, archived, and closed jobs never are. A job is scored once against each CV, by its short name (the file name without its extension and a leading `CV_`), and keeps its scores when its status changes. A CV added to `cvs` or renamed counts as a new CV: each job you're pursuing is scored against it the next time you score or tailor it, and keeps its other scores. Scores against a CV no longer named in `cvs` are kept in the database but neither shown nor used. A CV over 28,000 characters (as text) isn't scored, tailored, or written from at all, rather than worked on from its start; `interview-prep` alone uses the start of it, and warns.

The CVs are the 1 or 2 files `cvs` names at the top of `config/profile.toml` (2 at most, as each is one LLM call per job). `tailor-cv`, `cover-letter`, `skill-gaps`, and `interview-prep` use only those; until `cvs` names them correctly, the tracker says what's wrong and those commands stop.

Editing a CV doesn't rescore anything by itself: the tracker marks each score made against an earlier version of the CV, and **Rescore edited CVs** scores the jobs you're pursuing again against only the CVs you've edited (the job panel's **Rescore** does one job); closed jobs keep their scores. For a YAML CV only its `cv` section is scored, so only a change there counts as an edit, not a RenderCV design, locale, or settings change; for a `.pdf`, `.docx`, or `.tex`, only a change to its text (in a `.tex`, outside comments and commands).

## Tailored CVs

`tailor-cv` drafts from the job's best-matching RenderCV YAML CV (a CV in another format is scored, but can't be tailored; an unscored job is scored first), or the one `--cv` names. Only the CV's `cv` section goes to the LLM; design, locale, and settings are copied from your file. `CV_REVIEW.md` (with a `CV_REVIEW.docx` copy) holds the fact check, the reviewer's critique, and any bullets cut to fit the page limit. To render the YAML again after you edit it, pass `--pdf-path` as below: without it, RenderCV writes a new PDF to `rendercv_output/` and leaves the old one beside the draft. The draft is then compared with your original CV. These count as errors, and the CV is not rendered as a PDF or written as `--format`:
- a changed, added, or removed header field (name, contact details, and so on; the headline may be reworded, but is checked for numbers and terms like the rest)
- new or altered employers, positions, dates, or education
- an entry whose employer, position, dates, and education details don't match one entry of the original together, such as an end date taken from a later job or one employer's title given to another
- a removed employer, role (each position and start date at an employer), or education entry
- an entry's location changed (it may be left out)
- numbers that aren't in the original, and, in an entry, numbers from another entry of the original (a "30%" from one job given to another); a number in the headline or a section without employers or dates, such as skills, only has to be somewhere in the original
- markup that isn't in the original and that RenderCV would pass to Typst rather than print as text: a command such as `#text(fill: white)[...]` (text hidden in the PDF), `$$ math $$`, or a Markdown link, whose address the PDF doesn't show. A job ad could steer the LLM into adding one.

New capitalised or technical terms, such as a tool name, are warnings. The first word of a sentence or bullet is capitalised anyway, so it counts only when it starts a list ("Kubernetes, Python, SQL"), looks technical (AWS, PyTorch, Node.js), or is a term from the job ad, the likeliest to be claimed without support. The check catches common problems; it does not replace reading the draft.

With `--format docx` or `latex`, the CV file is laid out in code from the checked YAML (`candidate/documents.py`), never by the LLM, so it says exactly what passed the check. Like RenderCV, these layouts never split an entry across pages. A draft RenderCV can't render (the LLM wrote YAML it rejects, say) gets no PDF or `--format` file either, as its length was never checked, and like a draft with errors it is drafted again by the next `tailor-cv` unless you've edited it. A draft with errors gets no PDF and no `--format` file: fix the YAML and render it yourself with `uv run rendercv render CV_<variant>_draft.yaml --pdf-path CV_<variant>_draft.pdf` in its folder (a `.tex` compiles with lualatex). For a job tailored before, `--format docx` or `latex` just adds that file, laid out from its YAML draft after checking it again (in case you edited it), with no LLM call; if you've edited the draft since that file was written, it's written again. An edit is told by the draft's content, not its file times: tailor-cv records what it wrote in `.tailor-cv.json` in the job's folder, so copying the folder, or saving the draft unchanged, isn't an edit.

The length is always measured on RenderCV's PDF, which is written beside the YAML draft, so a `.docx` or `.tex` is fitted to RenderCV's layout and its own page count may differ. If that PDF runs past 1 page, or the `--max-pages N` given to `tailor-cv`, the LLM ranks the bullets from least to most relevant to the job, and the fewest of the least relevant that makes it fit are cut, found by re-rendering with different numbers cut, in a temporary folder (a binary search, so about 9 renders even for 50 bullets). If it can't fit even with every entry down to one bullet, all those cuts are kept and you get a warning. The LLM only ranks; the cutting is done in code, so nothing is rewritten and every entry keeps at least one bullet. `CV_REVIEW.md` lists what was cut.

Since an entry is never split, one too long for the space left moves whole to the next page. A page other than the last that is at least 30% empty at the bottom because of this gets a warning (for RenderCV's layout); cutting a few of that entry's bullets pulls it up.

## Cover letters and the review

The cover letter is written from your best-matching CV, in any format (`--cv` picks another), and may draw only on that CV, the job ad, and the company research. A number found in none of them is an error; a new capitalised or technical term is a warning, as is a `[placeholder]` left in. A letter with errors is not written as `cover-letter.docx`; its text is in `COVER_LETTER_REVIEW.md` to fix. The `.docx` is a standard business letter: your details, the date, the employer, a subject line, then the letter. Its header takes your name and contact details from the CV: from a RenderCV YAML CV, its name, location, email, and phone; from another format, the name the letter signs off with and the first email address and phone number in the CV. Cover letters are optional (`jobflow cover-letter`). For both CVs and letters, a reviewer prompted as the job's hiring manager critiques the first draft and revises it. The revision is checked the same way and kept only if it has fewer errors than the first draft, or as many errors and no more warnings (each new term is one warning), so it can't slip in unsupported terms while its errors stay level (a revision with fewer errors is kept even if it adds terms, which are listed as warnings); otherwise the first draft is kept and the review file (`CV_REVIEW.md` or `COVER_LETTER_REVIEW.md`) says so. A review that fails (a network error, say) keeps the first draft, and the review file says so; but if the reviewer's key or model is wrong, or its credit has run out, the command stops, rather than write an unreviewed draft for every job.

## Skill gaps

It is built from your stored scores, with one LLM call. Each scored job you're pursuing now contributes the requirements its best-matching CV didn't fully show; jobs still in the Inbox, and ones rejected, withdrawn, declined, or accepted, don't count. The LLM only groups them into skills (the same skill worded differently) and suggests how to close each gap; it can't change the counts. A skill's rank is the sum over its requirements of 1 for required or 0.5 for preferred, halved when the CV partly shows it. Ids the LLM makes up or repeats are ignored. Scores aren't redone by themselves, so the report and the command say how many of its jobs were scored against an earlier version of a CV: click **Rescore edited CVs** after adding a skill to your CV.

## Which jobs the commands do

`tailor-cv` and `cover-letter` work through every Interested job, and `interview-prep` every Interviewing one, or only the jobs `--job` names (repeat it for several). `--job` also takes a job you're pursuing past Interested, for a letter for a second round, say; `interview-prep` takes only Interviewing jobs. They do two jobs at a time, printing each job's output together once it's done, and check your LLM (and, for `interview-prep`, Tavily) settings before starting any. Only one run of each can go at a time: a second (in another terminal, say) stops, saying the first is still running. With more than 5 jobs to do, a run in a terminal asks before it starts (one naming its jobs with `--job` doesn't).

A job is done once its run finished and its output is still in its folder: its `CV_<variant>_draft.yaml` with its `CV_REVIEW.md`, its `cover-letter.docx`, or its `interview-prep.html`. So these are done again by the next run:
- a job whose run failed (a network error, say);
- a job whose output you deleted (with `clean-up`, say). Deleting only a CV's PDF doesn't count: render it again from the YAML;
- a CV or letter that failed the fact check, or a CV RenderCV couldn't render, unless you've fixed it yourself: edited the `CV_<variant>_draft.yaml`, or saved the letter as `cover-letter.docx` in its folder. Then your version is kept.

`--job ID --redo` does a job again anyway, replacing its files, a fix of yours included. A draft that fails the check never leaves an older PDF, `.docx`, or `cover-letter.docx` beside it.

## The tracker

- **Minimum salary filter:** each job's salary is converted to US dollars at the fixed rates in `src/jobflow/ingest/options.py` (`USD_PER_UNIT`) and compared by the top of its range. A job without a salary, in a country whose currency isn't known, or whose ad gives no pay period (its salary guessed from the amount, shown with `~`; see [searches.md](searches.md#salary-in-different-currencies)) is always kept.
- **Rows shown:** up to 2,000 jobs per query; narrow the filters to find jobs beyond that. Export CSV (All) has every job in the tab, however many.
- **Flags on a row:** a follow-up date in the past, on a job not yet closed; an application still at Applied three weeks after you applied; a similar job you've already applied for ([similar jobs](#similar-jobs)).
- **Flags in the header:** no import has succeeded for 36 hours; a site's runs failed in the last 36 hours, even if other sites' succeeded; a site's runs returned all the results `max_results` allows in that time, so probably left jobs behind (raise `max_results`, or narrow the search). The searches look back a day ([when the import runs](#when-the-daily-import-runs)), so a day the import missed is jobs never seen.
- **Saving:** changes are drafts until you save, and a save is all or nothing. If a job was changed elsewhere since the page loaded it, nothing is saved and the page shows what changed; saving again goes over it. The page refreshes itself every 3 minutes and when you come back to it, but never while you have unsaved drafts.

## Clean-up

`jobflow clean-up` deletes, after you confirm, the output folders of Not interested, Withdrawn, and Archived jobs you never applied for. A job you applied for keeps its folder, with the CV and letter you sent, whatever its status; Rejected and Declined jobs always count as applied for. The jobs, notes, and history stay in the database. A job whose status changes while you decide keeps its folder.

## Database tables

Every command except `init`, `help`, `status`, and `update-searches --dry-run` brings the database tables up to date when it starts, one command at a time.

## When the daily import runs

A cron service you set up starts the import each night at the time you choose, through GitHub's API ([setup](setup.md#7-start-the-daily-import-on-time)): late in the evening, because Seek's 1-day search returns only the jobs listed that day (in the listing country's timezone), so a run must start shortly before midnight to see the whole day. GitHub's own schedule starts runs hours late (in the morning, from its 00:17 Sydney cron), so it is a fallback: a scheduled run imports nothing when a run started on time (or with **Run workflow**) succeeded in the last 20 hours, and imports as usual otherwise, after a night the cron service missed or whose run failed. LinkedIn's 1-day window is searched as 30 hours and Indeed's as its last day, whenever the run starts ([searches.md](searches.md#posted-date-window)).

## Failures

If one Apify run fails, the error is recorded in the `scraper_runs` table, with the search name and the exact input, and the other runs still go ahead, unless the error is your Apify account's (a token Apify rejects, or the account's monthly usage limit reached): every run would fail the same way, so the runs not yet started are skipped. A run that succeeded on Apify but whose results couldn't be saved is recorded as `IMPORT_FAILED`, with its dataset ID: the results are still on Apify, already paid for, and `jobflow ingest --retry-imports` imports them (`jobflow status` says when there are any). Each is saved as it would have been on the day it ran, judged by the search that ran it; one whose search you've since renamed or removed is saved unfiltered, for `update-searches` to judge by `[defaults]`. Apify deletes a run's results after a while (how long depends on your plan): a run whose results are gone is recorded as `EXPIRED` and not tried again. A single posting that can't be read or that the database rejects is skipped (and named in the log) rather than failing the rest of its run; if every posting in a run fails, the run is recorded as `IMPORT_FAILED` instead. A run still going after `APIFY_MAX_WAIT_MINUTES` is stopped, so it can't keep charging for results that won't be imported; Apify stops it by itself too, so that holds even if the import is cut off (GitHub stops the daily job after 180 minutes). So that never happens, no run is started that could still be going 150 minutes after the import began (the 30 minutes left are for saving the last results and the upkeep): such a run isn't started, and is recorded as `NOT_STARTED` (each site's runs go one after another, so a site with many terms and places is the likeliest to reach it). The GitHub workflow then shows as failed, so you get GitHub's failure email.
